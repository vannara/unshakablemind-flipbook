Before you begin

A reflection

Do you believe you have to sacrifice your health, your family, or your peace to achieve your dream?

Do you treat rest and sleep as a reward you earn only after you achieve something?

If yes, and you don't want to change that, close this book. It is not for you.

If no, but you are not ready to change the way you work, the way you move, the way you are, stop reading here too.

This book is only for those who know their dream doesn't require sacrifice. Who know rest is not the reward. It is the fuel. Who are hungry for more, and ready to leave their old identity behind.

Because this is not a mindset trick. It is a new operating system, installed at the cellular level.

The story

From Reactive to Unshakable

All my life, all I knew about success was a comfortable life, a steady job, and a great salary. To get that, I had to work hard by sacrificing my freedom and happiness. No one in my circle taught me otherwise. I had to learn it the hard way.

I left a stable job in France and came home to Cambodia for freedom and happiness with my two-year-old daughter. I was a high achiever and a single mother, building my first business, when the country was hit by a crisis I did not see coming. Pressure from every side: time, money, stability. I worked nonstop, the same way I had in my corporate job. My nervous system was always on. I burned out. Worse: I became the kind of mother I hated myself for being. I screamed when I was annoyed and when she did not listen. I promised not to do it again. I kept doing it.

So I stopped building completely. I could not let her inherit this reaction. I was less reactive, but the same trigger still ran me.

The story

From Reactive to Unshakable

That led me to the patterns we inherit. Our body remembers what our mind does not. I studied how reactions work, and how we rewire them. The way I worked, the beliefs I carried, the reactions I made were inherited and conditioned.

I stopped building completely. But I didn't do nothing. Every day I sat in stillness, accessed alpha state, and started reprogramming my mind. Three weeks later, without looking for anything, the right people and resources started arriving. That was when I found the teachings that took everything deeper. I kept rewiring — my identity, my state, my mornings. Little by little I became less reactive, until one day the same trigger didn't shake me anymore. What I want now comes to me without forcing.

I generated my first income one month after launching my community. I got funded on my vision within four months of setting the intention. The state we hold is the foundation of everything we build.

This book is that protocol. Not a new personality. A daily way back to the state that can hold the life you want.

The sequence

The Unshakable Mind

Seven steps. One word. One day.

  1. R — Reprogram: upgrade your operating system before the world wakes up.
  2. E — Empty: unload everything consuming your time, energy and focus.
  3. W — Weigh: separate the non-negotiable from the negotiable, the focus from the on-the-go.
  4. I — Imagine: picture what success looks like at the end of your day.
  5. R — Rank: rank your top 3 actions that create 80% of the results.
  6. E — Execute: protect your time. Follow through, one thing at a time.
  7. D — Detach: surrender the outcome. Go to bed in peace.

How to enter

You do not do all seven at once.

Notice how you feel. The feeling tells you which step to open.

  • Tense? Reprogram.
  • Head too full? Empty, then Weigh.
  • No clear picture of success? Imagine, then Rank.
  • Pulled away? Execute.
  • Spiraling, or the day still open? Detach.

One feeling. One step. That is enough for today.

R · Reprogram

Upgrade before the world wakes up

Do you wake up and reach for your phone, already stressed before your day begins? What do you normally do?

What science says:

When you start the day stressed, your nervous system plans from survival mode, and stress narrows what you can see. Slow breathing activates your calming system and improves heart rate variability, a marker of calm and resilience. You plan from safety, not panic.

The practice:

Breathe and smile for heart coherence. Write your power statement and say it with elevated feeling. Move your body to seal the state.

Meditation can be eyes closed in stillness, or eyes open while cooking, cleaning, walking — fully present with what is in front of you. Both access the same receptive state. Choose what your morning allows.

Example

I am calm, clear, and I create my day.

E · Empty

Unload what is consuming you

Does your head feel so full that nothing else can fit? When you feel overloaded, what do you normally do?

What science says:

Your working memory holds only about four things at once (Cowan, 2001). Unfinished tasks keep looping in your mind — the Zeigarnik effect — and that loop drains your energy and focus. Writing down a plan for unfinished tasks stops them from intruding (Masicampo & Baumeister, 2011). When paper holds the list, your brain is free to think.

The practice:

Write every task and responsibility on paper. Nothing stays in your head.

Example

Launch page. Invoice follow-up. School pickup. Unread thread. Groceries. Accountant call. The idea I keep rehearsing. Sleep.

W · Weigh

Separate what matters

When too many things land on you at once, how do you know what to focus on next? What do you normally do?

What science says:

Research on the urgency effect shows people choose urgent tasks over important ones, even when the important ones pay off more (Zhu et al., 2018). Weighing breaks that bias, so urgency stops choosing for you.

The practice:

Split the list into non-negotiable and negotiable. Then tag each task as continuous or deadline, and as focus or on-the-go.

Example

Non-negotiableNegotiable
Launch page — deadline, focusUnread thread — on-the-go
School pickup — continuousGroceries — on-the-go
Sleep — continuousAccountant call — can move

I · Imagine

Picture the successful day

At the end of the day, how do you know you were successful? How do you know you moved closer to your goal, your dream?

What science says:

"I'll do my best" gives your brain no target. Goal-setting research by Locke and Latham, across hundreds of studies, shows that specific, clear goals lead to higher performance than vague ones.

The practice:

Write a concrete picture of what success looks like when your day ends.

Example

Task finished, and present with my loved ones at dinner.

R · Rank

The three that move the day

Are you spending your day reacting to your emotions and to people's complaints? Or are you deciding who you are, what you want, and the action that takes you there?

What science says:

When you decide in the moment, your emotions and other people's demands decide for you. Deciding in advance is an implementation intention. A review of 94 studies found it strongly increases follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).

The practice:

The night before, rank your top 3 actions that create 80% of the results. Fill the VPI table — Vision, Purpose, Inspired Action. Tomorrow, you don't decide. You execute.

Example

Vision — launch page sent; dinner present.

Purpose — I build from a steady body.

E · Execute

Protect the time. One thing.

Does your day get eaten by messages, calls, and interruptions? Do you jump between five tasks and finish none?

What science says:

It can take around 23 minutes to fully refocus after an interruption (Gloria Mark). Even a phone lying face-down nearby reduces your thinking capacity (Ward et al., 2017). Every time you switch tasks, you lose time and accuracy (Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans, 2001). Part of your attention stays stuck on the last task — attention residue (Sophie Leroy).

The practice:

Block the time. Put focus tasks in protected blocks and on-the-go tasks in the gaps. Phone out of the room. Then one thing at a time, in a primed state.

Example

From 9 to 11, I write the offer. Phone in another room. Nothing else.

D · Detach

Surrender the outcome. Sleep in peace.

Does one worry replay in your head again and again? What is the last thing you think, feel, or watch before you sleep?

What science says:

Replaying the same worry is strongly linked to stress and anxiety. Putting a feeling into words calms the amygdala (Lieberman et al., 2007). As you relax toward sleep, alpha brain waves increase and your mind becomes more open, so whatever you feed it then goes deeper. Gratitude before sleep is linked to better sleep quality (Wood et al., 2009).

The practice:

Day: ask, can I control this? Yes — next step. No — not mine to carry. Partly — only your part. Night: release whether the vision came or not. Read your power statement out loud. Feel it as already true. Fall asleep inside that feeling.

Example

I am grateful. I am unshakable. Tomorrow already supports my dream.

Tonight you detach. Tomorrow you reprogram. The loop is the method.

The identity

Executive mode as home

Repeat the move until it is not a morning trick. It is who you are when nothing special is happening.

Rewiring identity is not a speech in the mirror. It is a nervous system that defaults to choice instead of chase. Science gives you the levers: state, attention, one action. Spirit gives you the reason: you are allowed to create a life instead of surviving one.

When force appears, you have left the move. Notice the feeling. Make the one move it is asking for.
